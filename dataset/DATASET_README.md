# Decision AI — Simulated Dataset

Generated by `generate_dataset.py` (seed=42).

## Files
- `products.csv` — 22 products across 5 categories
- `transactions.csv` — 6051 SALE/RESTOCK events over 300 days
  (2025-01-01 to 2025-10-27)
- `dataset_summary.json` — machine-readable summary of the above

## Train / Holdout split
- **Train:** all days before 2025-09-28
- **Holdout:** last 30 days — use this to backtest your prediction
  engine (pretend you don't know it yet, predict, then check against these
  real recorded transactions).

## Personas included
- **steady**: 8 products
- **fast_mover**: 5 products
- **seasonal**: 4 products
- **slow_mover**: 4 products
- **cold_start**: 1 products

## Engineered demo scenarios (intentional, for your live demo & backtesting)
1. **Urgent reorder right now** — `SNK-001` (Potato Chips Classic)
   has its final stock deliberately trimmed to ~3-4 days of runway, so your
   dashboard has an immediately urgent product to show on day one.
2. **Historical stockout** — `HH-001` (Paper Towels 2-pack)
   was forced to zero available stock on days 200-
   212. Use this to prove your model *would have*
   flagged the risk in advance (backtesting story).
3. **Promotion spike** — `SNK-002` (Chocolate Bar) has a 3x demand
   spike on days 100-106, unrelated to
   any real trend. Use this to test whether your model wrongly overreacts to
   a one-time blip.
4. **Cold start** — `BEV-007` (New Flavor Soda) only has
   ~8 days of history, introduced near the end of the timeline. Use this to
   honestly demonstrate (and state as a known limitation) how your model
   handles a brand-new product with insufficient history.
5. **Slow movers** — 4 products (`SNK-003`, `BEV-005`, `HH-004`, `APP-001`)
   have near-zero, highly intermittent daily sales, deliberately reproducing
   the "sparse/intermittent demand" challenge flagged as a real drawback for
   small-business forecasting.

## Known realistic messiness baked in
- ~1.5% of days per product have **no sales record at all** (not zero sales —
  genuinely missing, as real POS exports often are).
- Restocks arrive with **random delay jitter** (0-3 extra days), simulating
  real supplier lead-time variability.

## Data dictionary

### products.csv
| column | meaning |
|---|---|
| sku | unique product identifier |
| name | product name |
| category | product category |
| unit_cost | cost per unit |
| current_stock | stock on hand as of the end of the generated timeline |
| reorder_lead_time_days | days a restock takes to arrive once ordered |
| persona | which sales pattern this product was generated with (for your own reference; not something your model should see as an input) |

### transactions.csv
| column | meaning |
|---|---|
| sku | product this transaction belongs to |
| date | date of the transaction |
| quantity | units sold or restocked |
| type | SALE or RESTOCK |
| split | "train" or "holdout" |

## Reproducing / adjusting
Change `SEED`, `TOTAL_DAYS`, or add/edit entries in the `PRODUCTS` list to
regenerate a different (but equally realistic) dataset. Everything is
deterministic given the same seed.
